## SolarIQ — Smart Energy Scheduler
A solar energy forecasting and smart appliance-scheduling dashboard, built on top of a comparative study of 5 time-series forecasting models.

This project implements the system described in the research paper:
"A Comparative Analysis of Time-Series Forecasting Models for Solar Energy Prediction in EV Charging and Smart Home Energy Management"
— Anisha S. (PG Scholar, M.Sc. Data Science), A. Santhoshi (Assistant Professor), Hindustan Institute of Technology and Science.

## What it does

Predicts solar irradiance and recommends the best time of day to run home appliances (EV charging, washing machine, AC, water heater) or charge an EV — based on forecasted solar generation — to save money and reduce grid dependency.

## Models compared

Five forecasting models were trained and evaluated on solar irradiance data (Pune, India):

XGBoost performed best, consistent with the paper's findings, and is the model powering this app.

Note: the numbers above come from running train_model.py on the included demo dataset (a public Kaggle-style solar plant dataset, resampled to hourly). Units are normalized irradiance (0–1 scale), not the raw W/m² scale used in the paper's original results — the model ranking (XGBoost best) is consistent across both.

## Tech stack

Frontend: HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Chart.js


## How the app works

The trained XGBoost model forecasts the next 24 hours of solar irradiance from the last known values (auto-regressive forecasting). Since the underlying research models are univariate time-series forecasters (matching the paper's methodology), the dashboard's weather inputs (cloud cover, temperature, wind speed) are applied as a simple rule-based adjustment on top of the model's forecast, not as direct model features — this is explained in detail in app.py's docstring.
